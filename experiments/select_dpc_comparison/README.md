# Atlas-DeePC / Select-DPC Comparisons

## Native Select-DPC data with Atlas graph waypoints

`native_data_graph_waypoints/` uses the exact IID Reacher trajectories shipped
inside `choose-wisely-paper-main.zip` and the original Select-DPC settings. The
online environment, seed 0 initial state, target `[0.043, 0.092]`, and all 100
physical waypoint references are identical to the Atlas rollout. Only the
offline datasets are method-specific in this condition.

| Method | Median total compute | p95 total compute | QPs | Final target error | Final-30 success |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | :---: |
| Atlas-DeePC | 26.9 ms | 35.7 ms | 100 | 1.82 mm | yes |
| Select-DPC, native data | 6,562.3 ms | 6,716.2 ms | 500 | 73.38 mm | no |

Native-data Select-DPC is 243.7x slower by median end-to-end latency. Its final
refinement reports `user_limit` on 98/100 steps and `optimal` on two. Its native
data improve final error over the shared-data condition (73 mm versus 217 mm),
but do not make the graph-waypoint task successful. This is specifically a
graph-reference compatibility result, not a reproduction of the paper's own
setpoint scheduler. Run it with `src/select_dpc_native_data_graph_waypoints.py`.

## Primary: shared data and graph waypoints

`shared_data_waypoints/` is the primary native-formulation comparison requested
for the Reacher waypoint task. Both methods use the exact same 200 underlying
offline trajectories, seed 0, target `[0.043, 0.092]`, initial simulator state,
100-step rollout, and causal physical waypoint schedule. Atlas generates the
schedule with its graph router; the schedule is then replayed unchanged to
Select-DPC after the exact 10-output-to-8-output coordinate permutation.

The methods intentionally retain their native formulations. Atlas uses its
10-output, `T_ini=2`, `T_f=10`, rank-60 graph-routed chart DeePC with CLARABEL.
The unchanged supplied Select-DPC source uses 8 outputs, `T_past=2`, `T_fut=15`,
200 raw-L1-selected columns, five refinements, its published costs and
constraints, and OSQP. The common Atlas Hankel library has 37,800 length-12
windows. Its ordered overlaps reconstruct the 200 length-200 trajectories with
zero numerical error; Select-DPC then builds 36,800 native length-17 windows
from those same trajectories.

| Method | Median total compute | p95 total compute | QPs | Final target error | Final-30 success |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | :---: |
| Atlas-DeePC | 26.9 ms | 35.7 ms | 100 | 1.82 mm | yes |
| Select-DPC | 6,582.4 ms | 7,029.6 ms | 500 | 217.08 mm | no |

Select-DPC's median end-to-end latency is 244.5x Atlas's. Atlas reports 100/100
optimal solves. Select-DPC's final refinement reports `user_limit` on 88/100
steps, `optimal_inaccurate` on four, and `optimal` on eight. This is consistent
with a tuning/distribution mismatch: the shared Atlas IID data spans the full
Reacher action range, whereas the Choose-Wisely notebook collected its native
offline data with actions in `[-0.1, 0.1]`. We do not retune Select-DPC because
the requested experiment keeps its original controller formulation.

The original code's open-loop fallback eventually takes an empty slice after
repeated non-optimal refinements. The runner adds only a disclosed safety guard
that resets this exhausted buffer to the code's documented zero-input fallback;
it does not alter selection, QPs, solver settings, or successful actions.

Run with `src/atlas_select_shared_waypoint_comparison.py`. The historical
native-formulation results and plots remain in `shared_data_waypoints/`. The
project-root `deepc_algorithm_comparison.ipynb` now presents the cleaner
controlled three-way selector comparison; it is generated by
`src/deepc_algorithm_comparison.py`.

## Secondary: fully matched formulation ablation

This directory contains a computational and closed-loop comparison between the
project's graph-routed Atlas-DeePC controller and Select-DPC as defined by the
user-supplied `choose-wisely-paper-main.zip` source archive.

## Secondary matched protocol

Both controllers use:

- the same frozen `data/select_iid_offline_hankel.npy` library;
- `Reacher-v5` with identical environment seeds and 320 control steps;
- the project's 10-dimensional output, `T_ini=2`, and `T_f=10`;
- identical static goal construction, costs, input bounds, regularization, and
  CLARABEL solver;
- the same strict final-30 and final-100 dwell criteria.

The method-specific differences are intentional. Select-DPC performs raw-L1
selection over the complete predicted input-output trajectory, retains 100
Hankel columns, and refines the prediction through five selection/DPC solves per
control step. Atlas-DeePC localizes in a frozen 64-chart atlas, routes to the
goal chart, and performs one rank-60 current-chart DeePC solve per step.

This protocol preserves Select-DPC's defining selection and sequential
linearization mechanism while removing confounds from the paper notebook's
different output vector, horizon, input bound, costs, and solver. The exact
source archive and the two relevant source-file hashes are recorded in every
comparison `summary.json`.

## Timing definitions

- **Selection/localization**: full-library L1 ranking for Select-DPC; 64-center
  chart localization for Atlas-DeePC.
- **Matrix preparation**: extracting the selected columns or retrieving/building
  the active chart basis.
- **QP build + solve**: construction, canonicalization, and solve of every DeePC
  QP. Select-DPC totals all refinement iterations at the control step.
- **Total control compute**: all controller work before stepping MuJoCo. Simulator
  stepping and plotting are excluded.

This secondary comparison is implemented by `src/select_dpc_comparison.py`.

## Current frozen paired result

The predeclared first-five held-out audit uses seeds 111 through 115, 100
selected columns, five Select-DPC refinements, and 320 control steps.

| Method | Strict successes | Median of per-seed median latency | Median of per-seed p95 latency | QPs per seed |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Graph-routed Atlas-DeePC | 5/5 | 32.0 ms | 57.5 ms | 320 |
| Matched Select-DPC | 0/5 | 871.9 ms | 1242.6 ms | 1600 |

The median-latency ratio is 27.3x. Select-DPC seed 111 is a near miss rather
than a total control failure: it passes the final-30 test but its final-100 mean
is 10.19 mm, just above the 10 mm threshold. The other four Select-DPC runs fail
both strict dwell tests. All 9,600 QPs across both methods report `optimal`.

The five-seed audit supports a matched computational comparison and an initial
control-reliability observation. It is not a 100-seed Select-DPC evaluation and
must not be presented as one.
